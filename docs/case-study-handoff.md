## Cole West

**Role:** Marketing Manager

# Cole West

### Connecting marketing and sales

I ran campaigns for new communities and move-in-ready homes, then built tools that helped sales and leadership work from the same information.

**Hero:** Use the Cole West Territory billboard from the existing project assets or page 4 of the marketing portfolio. Keep the artwork in its original wide proportions.

### From verbal offers to a written approval process

I built a form that calculated offer prices and concessions and turned the submission into a PDF for review. The sales team and reviewers could work from the same numbers and refer back to what was submitted.

**Offer walkthrough:** Use clearly labeled fictional figures. Start with a $500,000 base price, $25,000 lot premium, $20,000 in options, and a $10,000 price incentive. The final contract price is $535,000. Show the last step below the total: generate a PDF and route it for review. If interactive, recalculate as inputs change and simulate the review step entirely on the page.

### Shared information, clearer decisions

- **Sales release.** I gave the division one reference for available homes, options, and pricing.
- **Construction starts.** I built a planning tool the construction team used in weekly reviews to decide which buildings to start across communities.
- **Executive reporting.** I built a Looker Studio dashboard leadership used to review marketing performance each week.

### Homes of the Week

I used email, digital ads, and billboards to help buyers discover communities and find homes ready to move into.

**Creative:** Use the Homes of the Week email from page 2 of the marketing portfolio. Give it a readable full-size view through an accessible expand control or image link. Use campaign creative and fictional sample data in the public page.

### What Cole West taught me about data

Building these tools changed how I approach marketing. Before I use a report to make a decision, I want to understand where the numbers came from, what might be missing, and whether the people using them understand them the same way. Inaccurate data can make a bad decision feel well supported.

At Cole West, that meant making it easier for agents to submit accurate offers, giving the sales team one place to check pricing, and building reports leadership could use in a weekly meeting. I still bring that mindset to marketing today. I start by asking what decision we’re trying to make, what information we need to make it, and how we’ll know we can trust that information.

## BlueBolt Electric

**Role:** Brand identity and web design

# BlueBolt Electric

When Spencer went out on his own, he wanted to grow into permanent lighting alongside his electrical work. He wanted BlueBolt to feel modern and polished, and he needed a website where customers could find him, see what he offered, and reach out about a job.

**Hero:** Show the BlueBolt homepage screenshot in a simple browser frame. The screenshot is on page 3 of the marketing portfolio. Add a [Visit BlueBolt Electric](https://bluebolteelectric.com) link next to the intro.

### A brand with something personal in it

I designed the logo and color palette and built the website. One of my favorite parts of the project is the dog in the logo. It’s based on Spencer’s own dog, and it gave the new brand something personal from day one.

**Identity artwork:** Use the original logo in the right-hand panel when available. A real photo of Spencer’s dog can sit beside it if supplied. The mockup uses a placeholder for the original artwork.

### What I’d ask today

This was early in my marketing career. If I sat down with Spencer today, I’d spend more time asking how he wants customers to connect with him and what they need to see before they feel ready to reach out. Those answers would shape both the story the site tells and the next step it asks people to take.

**First comes the story, then comes the CTA.**
